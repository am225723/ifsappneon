Welcome to Inner Child Play.

This practice is an invitation to reconnect with playfulness, imagination, and joy.

You do not have to feel playful right away.

Sometimes playful parts are hidden behind protectors.

That is okay.

Begin by settling your body.

Take a gentle breath in and let it out.

Now notice any part of you that reacts to the idea of play.

Maybe a part feels excited.

Maybe a part feels silly.

Maybe a part feels embarrassed.

Maybe a part says, this is pointless.

All of that is welcome.

Now ask inside.

Is there a younger part of me that remembers play, or wishes it had more room to play?

If a younger part appears, notice what kind of play it is drawn toward, maybe drawing, dancing,

Building, running, singing, pretending, exploring, laughing, making something, being messy, being free.

Ask this part.

What kind of play did you love or wish you had?

Now imagine creating a safe play space for this part.

It might be a room full of art supplies, a backyard, a playground, a beach.

A pile of blankets A music room A magical world Let it be whatever this part wants Now ask What would you like to do here?

Imagine allowing this younger part to play without criticism.

No one is judging.

No one is rushing.

No one is grading the outcome.

If a critical or embarrassed part appears, gently acknowledge it.

You might say,

I see you.

You may be trying to protect me from feeling foolish.

You do not have to disappear, but maybe you can give this younger part a little room.

Now return to the younger part.

Let it have a few moments of play.

Notice what happens in your body as this part plays.

Maybe warmth.

Maybe sadness.

Maybe resistance.

Maybe lightness.

Maybe nothing obvious.

Whatever happens is okay.

Now ask the younger part.

And what is one small playful thing I could let into my real life?

It could be tiny.

A color.

A song.

A doodle.

A silly movement.

A few minutes outside.

A playful object.

A moment of imagination.

Now thank this younger part for showing you something Let them know play does not have to be earned Joy does not have to be perfect Take a slow breath in

and let it out return to the room around you.

And consider one small way to let play have a little space today.

Your playful parts do not need to prove their usefulness.

Joy, imagination, wonder, and silliness are not distractions from healing.

Sometimes they are part of how healing finds its way back into the body.
