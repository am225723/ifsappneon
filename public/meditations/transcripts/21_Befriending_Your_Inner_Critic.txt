Welcome to befriending your inner critic.

This practice is about turning toward the inner critic with curiosity instead of fighting it.

The goal is not to let the critic take over.

The goal is to understand what it is protecting.

Begin by settling your body.

Take a slow breath in.

and let it out.

Now gently bring to mind the inner critic.

Notice its voice, its tone, its favorite messages, its posture or image if it has one.

Do not argue with it yet, just notice it.

Ask, where do I sense this critic in or around my body?

Now ask, how do I feel toward this critic?

If you feel angry, ashamed, afraid, or exhausted, that makes sense.

See if those reactions can give you a little space so you can listen.

Now, say to the critic, I know you may be trying to help.

I'm not here to let you hurt me.

I am here to understand why you work so hard.

Ask the critic, what are you afraid would happen if you stop criticizing me?

Listen.

Maybe it fears failure, rejection, humiliation, abandonment, loss of control, being seen, being too much, or not being enough.

Now ask, who or what taught you that criticism was necessary?

Listen gently.

You do not need a full memory.

Just notice what comes.

Now ask, what are you trying to make me become?

And what are you trying to prevent me from becoming?

Notice the protective intention.

The critic may be harsh because it believes harshness keeps you safe.

Now say, I understand that you are trying to protect me.

I also need a different way.

Would you be willing to try speaking to me with more honesty and less attack?

Notice the response.

If the critic is unwilling, ask what it needs first.

If it is willing, invite it to rephrase one message.

For example, instead of you are a failure, it might say, I am scared you will be rejected.

Instead of you are too much, it might say, I am afraid people will leave.

Instead of you must be perfect, it might say, I need reassurance that mistakes are survivable.

Now thank the critic for revealing what is underneath.

Ask, what would help you trust self-energy more?

Listen.

Take a slow breath in and let it out.

Let the critic know you will keep listening, but you will also practice leading with compassion.

Return gently to the room.
