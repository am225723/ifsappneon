Welcome to the self leadership mastery practice.

This practice is about learning to lead your inner system with steadiness, respect and compassion.

Self leadership does not mean controlling your parts, it means listening from enough spaciousness that your parts do not have to take over completely.

Begin by settling your body, let your shoulders soften, let your jaw loosen, let your breath be natural.

Take a slow breath in and let it out.

Now bring to mind a recent moment when a part of you felt activated.

Choose something manageable, not the hardest thing.

Just a moment when a part took over, reacted, withdrew, criticized, protected, or pushed.

Notice the scene gently.

Where were you?

What happened?

What part showed up?

Now pause the scene.

Imagine you are watching it from a little distance.

Ask, which part of me was leading in that moment?

Maybe a manager, maybe a firefighter, maybe an inner critic, maybe a people pleaser, maybe an avoidant part, maybe a protector that wanted control.

Notice that part without judging it.

Now ask, what was this part trying to accomplish?

What was it protecting?

What was it afraid would happen?

Listen.

Now ask, how do I feel toward this part right now?

If you feel frustrated or ashamed, notice that another part may be present.

Ask that part for a little space too.

Self-leadership begins with noticing who is leading.

Now imagine stepping into the scene with more self-energy.

You do not erase the part.

You stand near it.

You might say to the part, I see that you were trying to help.

I understand that you felt something was at stake.

I am here now.

You do not have to handle this alone.

Now ask the part, what would you need from me in order to let me lead a little more next time?

Maybe it needs reassurance, a plan, protection, a boundary, a slower pace, more support or more honesty.

Now imagine the same situation again, but this time self is present.

Notice what changes.

Maybe your body feels different.

Maybe your tone changes.

Maybe you pause before responding.

Maybe you name what is happening.

Maybe you set a boundary.

Maybe you ask for time.

Maybe you do less, not more.

Let the new possibility be simple.

Now ask your system, what is one leadership phrase I can use when I notice a part taking over?

Examples might be, I see you.

Let me be with this.

We can slow down.

I can listen without letting you run the whole system.

You do not have to do this alone.

Choose one phrase that feels true.

Now imagine using that phrase during the next difficult moment.

Let your body feel what it would be like to pause, breathe, and lead from self-energy.

Take a slow breath in.

And let it out.

Thank the parts that showed up.

Thank the protectors.

Thank the part of you that is learning to lead.

When you are ready, return to the room.
